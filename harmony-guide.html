Color Harmony Guide

Understanding color relationships to create beautiful, balanced palettes

🎯Complementary

Opposite Attraction

Complementary colors sit directly opposite each other on the color wheel (180° apart). This creates the highest contrast and most vibrant pairing. Think of classic combinations like blue and orange, red and green, or yellow and purple.

Pro Tip:

Use one color as dominant and the other as accent. Avoid using them in equal amounts as this can feel too intense or vibrating.

△Triadic

Triangle Balance

Three colors evenly spaced around the color wheel (120° apart) form a triadic harmony. This creates a vibrant, balanced palette with rich contrast. Examples include red-yellow-blue (primary colors) or orange-green-purple (secondary colors).

Pro Tip:

Let one color dominate while using the other two as accents. This prevents the palette from feeling too busy or overwhelming.

🌊Analogous

Neighbors in Harmony

Colors that sit next to each other on the color wheel create an analogous harmony. These palettes feel natural, serene, and cohesive because they share similar hues. Think of a sunset with oranges, reds, and yellows, or an ocean scene with blues and greens.

Pro Tip:

Vary the lightness and saturation to add depth. An all-analogous palette can feel monotonous without tonal variety.

🎨Split-Complementary

Softer Opposition

Instead of using the direct complement, this harmony uses the two colors adjacent to the complement. This provides strong contrast like complementary colors, but with less tension and more nuance. It's easier to balance than pure complementary.

Pro Tip:

Use the base color as dominant and the split-complements as accents. This creates visual interest without overwhelming contrast.

◇Tetradic

Four-Corner Balance

Four colors arranged in two complementary pairs create a tetradic (or double-complementary) harmony. This offers rich, complex palettes with lots of variation. The square variant uses colors evenly spaced 90° apart for perfect balance.

Pro Tip:

Choose one color to dominate. With four colors, you risk creating chaos. Use the others sparingly for accents and highlights.

🎨Monochromatic

Shades of Unity

A monochromatic palette uses variations of a single hue—different shades (darker), tints (lighter), and tones (grayer) of the same color. This creates sophisticated, cohesive designs that feel elegant and unified.

Pro Tip:

Ensure strong lightness contrast between elements. Without hue variation, contrast comes from light and dark values.

💡 General Tips

Mastering Color Harmony

1. Lightness Matters: Good color harmony isn't just about hue relationships. Varying lightness (light vs. dark) is crucial for readability and visual hierarchy.

2. Saturation Balance: Mix vibrant and muted colors. All highly saturated colors can feel overwhelming, while all muted colors may feel dull.

3. The 60-30-10 Rule: Use 60% dominant color, 30% secondary color, and 10% accent color for balanced compositions.

4. Context is King: Colors behave differently depending on what's around them. Test your palette in its intended context.

5. Trust Your Eye: Color theory provides guidelines, not rules. If something feels right, it probably is!
